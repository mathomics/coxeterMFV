Coxeter-MFV

A free web app that draws metabolic models of organisms and microbial communities as cells and compartments, and runs flux balance analysis on them. Use it to explore a model, test media and knockouts, follow cross-feeding between species and make figures.

What you can do

Open your own model

SBML, COBRA JSON, MAT or Excel. Several models make a community.

Draw cells and compartments

Automatic layout, then move, reshape and add reactions, metabolites, compartments and cells.

Analyze

FBA, pFBA, FVA, pFVA, loopless FBA, CycleFreeFlux, SteadyCom and GIMME, with any objective.

Change conditions

Medium, bounds, reaction and gene knockouts, and species abundances.

Study communities

Metabolites exchanged between species, in the Cross-Feeding Explorer and on the map.

Add omics data

Gene expression, proteomics and metatranscriptomics, and GIMME context-specific models.

Export

Maps as SVG or PNG, fluxes as CSV or JSON, models as SBML or COBRA JSON, and a methods report.

Reproduce

Bundles of model, map, settings and solution, with checksums, that solve again when opened.

Offline and desktop

Download the single-file app (18 MB) to work offline. A Windows version, with HiGHS and optionally Gurobi or CPLEX, is in the repository.

FAQ

  1. What is Coxeter-MFV?

    A web app that draws metabolic models as maps of cells and compartments and runs flux balance analysis on them.

  2. Do I need to install anything or create an account?

    No. It runs in the browser, with no login.

  3. Are my models uploaded?

    No. Calculations run in your browser, and there is no cookie or tracking. The Yeast9 and phenylalanine rescue examples download public model files.

  4. Which files can I open?

    Models in SBML Level 3 with FBC, COBRA JSON, COBRA Toolbox MAT or Excel, maps and scenarios saved by Coxeter-MFV, fluxes as Escher reaction data or CSV, and omics tables as CSV or TSV.

  5. Can I open Escher maps?

    No. Coxeter-MFV reads and writes Escher's flux data, not its maps.

  6. How large can a model be?

    The automatic layout handles up to about 350 reactions per organism, and larger models start from core metabolism. Genome-scale models such as Yeast9, with 4105 reactions, solve in the browser.

  7. Which solver does it use?

    GLPK in the browser. The Windows version uses HiGHS, or Gurobi or CPLEX when installed.

  8. What browsers can I use?

    A current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.

  9. How do I cite Coxeter-MFV?

    Use CITATION.cff until the article is published.

  10. How is Coxeter-MFV licensed?

    The code is under the MIT License. The app includes glpk.js and is distributed under GPL-3.0. Bundled models keep their own licenses.

  11. Where do I report a problem?

    On GitHub issues.

About the name

Coxeter-MFV stands for Coxeter Multicell Flux Visualizer. The name honors the geometer Harold Scott MacDonald Coxeter (1907-2003), whose work on nested geometric structures and symmetry groups inspired the app's nested cells and compartments. His figures of hyperbolic tilings also inspired M. C. Escher's Circle Limit prints.
