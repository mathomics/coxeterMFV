Help

Coxeter-MFV draws a metabolic model as cells with nested compartments and solves it in your browser. The map shows part of the model, and every analysis solves the whole model.

Getting started

Open the app. A welcome window offers three case studies and Import a model. Close it to reach the editor, and reopen it with ? in the header.

  • The left panel has three tabs. Edit Map draws, Edit Model changes the network, medium and scenario, and Simulate chooses the analysis.
  • The map is in the middle. The Inspector on the right describes the selected reaction, metabolite or cell.
  • Below the map are Analysis results, Table view and Logs.

To solve, choose the analysis in the Simulate tab and press Simulate.

The three case studies open directly: Yeast9 compartments, phenylalanine rescue and auxotrophic community.

Your model

File, ImportReads
ModelSBML Level 3 with the FBC package, COBRA JSON, COBRA Toolbox MAT or Excel. Select several files at once to build a community: each model becomes a cell, and the cells share one extracellular medium.
Map JSONA saved Coxeter-MFV map. Import the model first so the map's identifiers can be matched to it.
Flux dataFluxes computed elsewhere, as Escher reaction data JSON or a CSV of reactions and fluxes.
MetatranscriptomicsExpression tables as CSV or TSV.

Without a map, the app lays out models of up to about 350 reactions per organism, and an overview of core metabolism for larger ones. You can then move, reshape and add elements; drawing never changes the model.

Sample files: the Yeast9 9.1.1 model (JSON, 3.4 MB) and its map of the cytoplasm, mitochondrion and peroxisome (JSON, 0.8 MB). Import the model, then the map.

Analyses

AnalysisWhat it computes
FBAA flux distribution that optimizes the objective, usually growth, at steady state within the bounds.
pFBAFixes the objective at a chosen fraction of its optimum, 100% by default, and minimizes total absolute flux.
FVAThe minimum and maximum flux of each reaction at the chosen objective requirement.
Loopless FBAFBA with constraints that exclude internal cycles.
CycleFreeFluxRemoves internal cycles from an FBA solution, keeping its objective and exchange fluxes.
SteadyComGrows all members of a community at one rate, μ, finds the largest feasible μ and predicts each member's abundance.
GIMMEBuilds a context-specific model from gene expression data.

Change the medium, bounds and reaction or gene knockouts from the map or under Edit Model, then solve again. The browser solves with GLPK.

An FBA optimum is usually one of many. Before reading much into an internal flux, compare it with pFBA, CycleFreeFlux or its FVA range.

Reading the map

Cells and compartments

  • Each organism is a cell with its own boundary and name plate. After an analysis, the plate shows the cell's growth rate, μ, in h⁻¹.
  • Colored regions are compartments, nested as in the cell, such as the mitochondrion inside the cytoplasm. The Legend, under Map options, lists their colors. A dashed outline marks a space that is not enclosed by a membrane, such as the shared medium.
  • A reaction drawn inside a compartment has all its metabolites there. A transport reaction sits on the membrane it crosses, and an exchange reaction on the cell boundary.
  • In a community, all cells sit in the shared medium, and metabolites pass between them through it.

Reaction lines

Color shows the size of the flux, from gray near zero through blue and violet to red. The scale is compressed so that a few very large fluxes do not wash out the rest. The Legend gives its values in mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹.

With Width by flux on, line width also grows with the flux.

The arrow shows the direction of flux in this solution. A reaction running backward points the other way.

Gray and dashed: no flux in this solution.

Pink and dashed: closed, with both bounds at 0, for example after a reaction or gene knockout.

The Color menu can also show absolute flux from white to red, or color by subsystem or pathway. The Labels menu chooses which reactions are labeled, and Cofactors shows or hides secondary metabolites such as ATP and NADH.

Flux ranges

With FVA on, each drawn reaction shows [minimum, maximum] after its name. Each bound comes from its own optimization, so the bounds of different reactions need not occur in the same solution. A range of a single value means the flux is the same in every optimum.

Communities

  • Cross-feeding, in the toolbar, highlights metabolites that pass between cells through the shared medium, one color per metabolite. The Legend lists them with their number of lines.
  • A solution says how much each cell secretes and takes up, not who feeds whom. The app splits each metabolite's transfer among producer and consumer pairs in proportion to their secretion and uptake, counting external supply as a producer and washout as a consumer.
  • The Cross-Feeding Explorer lists each transfer with its producer, consumer and flux, and filters them.
  • After SteadyCom, reaction lines are in mmol per gDW of that species per hour, and cross-feeding lines in mmol per gDW of the whole community per hour, which is abundance times flux.

Differences

In the phenylalanine rescue example, Δ flux vs WT monoculture colors each reaction by its change from the wild type, blue for a decrease and red for an increase.

Reactions that are not drawn

Every analysis uses the complete model, so reactions that are not on the map can still carry flux. In the reaction list, the Not on map filter shows them, and Active shows every reaction with flux.

Results panel

Analysis results reports the status, objective value, solver and computation time. Table view lists the reaction fluxes, and Logs keeps a record of each run.

Privacy

Every calculation runs in your browser. Models, maps and results are not uploaded. There is no login, no cookie and no tracking. The app keeps your work in this browser's storage, and a private window forgets it when closed. Links to databases such as BiGG or KEGG open only when you click them, and the phenylalanine rescue example downloads the authors' public model files.

Common problems

ProblemWhat to check
No feasible solutionThe medium, uptake bounds, objective, knockouts and maintenance requirement. For communities, also the abundances.
Internal fluxes change between runs or solversAlternative optima. Compare the objective values, and use pFBA or FVA.
The phenylalanine rescue does not loadIt needs an internet connection the first time, to download and verify the authors' models.
Something elseReport it on GitHub issues, with the browser and a small example. Avoid posting unpublished or personal data.

Coxeter-MFV runs in current desktop browsers and was tested in Chromium and Firefox. Download the single-file app to work offline.
