Help

Coxeter-MFV draws a metabolic model as a map of cells and compartments and solves it in your browser. The map shows part of the model, and every analysis solves the whole model.

Getting started

Open the app. A welcome window offers the three case studies of the article and Import a model. Open the editor closes it, ? in the top bar opens it again, and Show at startup stops it from opening each time. The case studies also open directly: Yeast9 compartments, phenylalanine rescue and auxotrophic community.

The screen

  • Top bar. Find searches every reaction of the model. ? opens the welcome window and ◐ switches between light and dark. File imports and exports, Simulate runs an analysis and Clear solution removes it from the map. The status shows the last analysis, and Saved shows that your work matches the last file saved or opened.
  • Left panel, in three tabs. Edit Map holds the drawing tools, Edit Model the scenario, species, medium, reactions, model and omics, and Simulate the analyses. ‹ collapses it and Focus mode gives the map more space.
  • Map toolbar. Show or hide the left panel, undo and redo map edits, zoom and fit the map, and choose the labels, line color, cofactors, cross-feeding lines and line width. Image exports the map, View holds further display options and Inspector shows or hides the right panel.
  • Right panel. Inspector describes the selected reaction, metabolite or cell and edits it. Layout sets the automatic layout and the canvas size.
  • Results panel, below the map: Analysis results, Table view and Logs.

Larger editors, such as Reactions & bounds or Map options, open as full pages over the map. ← Return to map or Esc closes them.

Without a map, the app lays out models of up to about 350 reactions per organism. Larger models start from an overview of core metabolism that you can extend. Drawing never changes the model.

Sample files: the Yeast9 9.1.1 model (JSON, 3.4 MB) and its map of the cytoplasm, mitochondrion and peroxisome (JSON, 0.8 MB). Import the model, then the map.

Editing the map

Map edits change only the drawing, never the model, and each can be undone.

Inspector

  • A reaction shows its equation, gene rule, genes, subsystem, annotations, flux, reduced costs and bounds. Bounds change with sliders or typed values, and Knockout closes the reaction. Under Map drawing, it can be curved or straight, aligned, mirrored, rotated, turned around, tidied or deleted from the map, and given its own label and color.
  • A metabolite shows its formula, charge, shadow price and reactions. It can be primary or secondary (smaller), recolored, duplicated to untangle lines, or hidden.
  • A cell shows its species, compartments, abundance and growth rate.
  • Several selected objects can be aligned, distributed, evenly spaced, rotated, or laid out as a circle with Circularize, as for the TCA cycle.

Map pages

The Layout tab, in the right panel, reruns the automatic layout with a preset (Compact, Normal or Spacious) or your own cell area, cell spacing, compartment size and node spacing, and sets the canvas size. For a model too large to lay out automatically, the presets keep a drawing made by hand.

Changing the model

These pages, under Edit Model, change the model or its conditions. Solve again to see the effect.

Edit Model pageUse
Scenario & objectiveChoose a scenario: a teaching scenario or a published community, with its source, its species' models (JSON or SBML), medium and bounds (CSV) and published results. Set the objective, and for a community solve SteadyCom at a fixed growth rate.
Species & abundanceEach species' abundance in the community, which sum to 1, and its compartments. Add species from model files.
Shared mediumThe uptake limit of each metabolite, in mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹. At 0 a metabolite is available only if a species secretes it.
Reactions & boundsEvery reaction, grouped by type, compartment, species, subsystem or pathway, and filtered by exchanges, modified, active, blocked, on or off the map, or knocked out. Edit bounds, knock out reactions or genes (through the gene rules), set the objective, undo changes, and import or export bounds as CSV.
Biological modelAdd metabolites and reactions to a species' model, see its size, and export it as COBRA JSON or SBML.
Omics & expressionOverlay gene expression or proteomics (a CSV with identifiers and two conditions) or metatranscriptomics, compare conditions, and run GIMME. Values reach reactions through the gene rules: the minimum for AND and the maximum for OR.

The objective can be any reaction with any coefficient, maximized or minimized. For a community, choose equal growth of all species (shared μ) or a weighted sum of their biomass reactions.

Analyses

Run an analysis from Simulate in the top bar or the Simulate tab. Simulation settings sets the objective, the fraction of the optimum pFBA keeps, loopless and FVA.

AnalysisWhat it computes
FBAA flux distribution that optimizes the objective, usually growth, at steady state within the bounds.
pFBAKeeps the objective at a chosen fraction of its optimum, 100% by default, and minimizes total absolute flux.
FVAThe minimum and maximum flux of each reaction at the chosen objective requirement. Run FBA, pFBA or Loopless first.
pFVAThe same ranges among solutions with the least total flux of pFBA.
Loopless FBAFBA with constraints that exclude internal cycles. Slower, as a mixed-integer problem.
CycleFreeFluxRemoves internal cycles from an FBA solution, keeping its objective and exchange fluxes.
SteadyComFor a community: all species grow at one rate, μ. Calculate range finds the feasible μ and Solve at this μ predicts each species' abundance. Under Scenario & objective.
GIMMEA context-specific solution that penalizes flux through reactions whose expression is below a threshold. Under Omics & expression.

The browser solves with GLPK. An FBA optimum is usually one of many: before reading much into an internal flux, compare it with pFBA, CycleFreeFlux or its FVA range.

Reading the map

Cells and compartments

  • Each organism is a cell with its own boundary and name plate. After an analysis, the plate shows the cell's growth rate, μ, in h⁻¹.
  • Colored regions are compartments, nested as in the cell, such as the mitochondrion inside the cytoplasm. The Legend, under Map options, lists their colors. A dashed outline marks a space that is not enclosed by a membrane, such as the shared medium.
  • A reaction drawn inside a compartment has all its metabolites there. A transport reaction sits on the membrane it crosses, and an exchange reaction on the cell boundary.
  • In a community, all cells sit in the shared medium, and metabolites pass between them through it.

Reaction lines

Color shows the size of the flux, from gray near zero through blue and violet to red. The scale is compressed so that a few very large fluxes do not wash out the rest. The Legend gives its values in mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹.

With Width by flux on, line width also grows with the flux.

The arrow shows the direction of flux in this solution. A reaction running backward points the other way.

Gray and dashed: no flux in this solution.

Pink and dashed: closed, with both bounds at 0, for example after a reaction or gene knockout.

Display options

  • Color colors lines by flux, by absolute flux from white to red, by pathway or by subsystem. Map options also offers your own color scale, and Pathway ribbons draw a translucent band behind the reactions of each subsystem or pathway.
  • Labels labels the reactions with major fluxes, all active reactions, all reactions or none. Under View, labels can show model IDs, names, EC numbers or database identifiers such as BiGG, KEGG or MetaNetX, when the model has them.
  • Cofactors shows or hides secondary metabolites such as ATP, NADH and H⁺. Under View, cofactors can also be labeled or drawn small and light.
  • Under View, Map view switches between the full map and core metabolism, and the scrolling choice sets whether the mouse wheel zooms or a touchpad pans.

Flux ranges

With FVA or pFVA on, each drawn reaction shows [minimum, maximum] after its name. Each bound comes from its own optimization, so the bounds of different reactions need not occur in the same solution. A range of a single value means the flux is the same in every optimum. In Reactions & bounds, Blocked lists the reactions whose range is 0 to 0.

Communities

  • Cross-feeding, in the toolbar, highlights metabolites that pass between cells through the shared medium, one color per metabolite. The Legend lists them with their number of lines. Under View, choose how many are shown, or pick them one by one, and set their line width.
  • A solution says how much each cell secretes and takes up, not who feeds whom. The app splits each metabolite's transfer among producer and consumer pairs in proportion to their secretion and uptake, counting external supply as a producer and washout as a consumer.
  • The Cross-feeding explorer, in the Simulate tab, lists each transfer with its producer, consumer and flux, and chooses which metabolites appear on the map.
  • After SteadyCom, reaction lines are in mmol per gDW of that species per hour, and cross-feeding lines in mmol per gDW of the whole community per hour, which is abundance times flux.

Differences

In the phenylalanine rescue example, Scenario & objective chooses the case to solve, and Flux display, Δ flux vs WT monoculture, colors each reaction by its change from the wild type, blue for a decrease and red for an increase.

Reactions that are not drawn

Every analysis uses the complete model, so reactions that are not on the map can still carry flux. In Reactions & bounds, Not on map shows them, and Active shows every reaction with flux.

Results

  • Analysis results reports the status, objective value, solver and computation time. Its tabs list the largest fluxes, the FVA ranges, the reduced costs, scaled and unscaled, the shadow prices, and the comparison of a reproducibility replay. Values the solver does not return are marked as such.
  • Table view lists every reaction's flux, and the shadow prices of the metabolites. Open in Reactions opens the full table.
  • Logs records each run with its time.
  • The Inspector shows the same values for the selected reaction or metabolite.

Privacy

Every calculation runs in your browser. Models, maps and results are not uploaded. There is no login, no cookie and no tracking. The app keeps your work in this browser's storage, and a private window forgets it when closed. Links to databases such as BiGG or KEGG open only when you click them. The Yeast9 and phenylalanine rescue examples download public model files.

Common problems

ProblemWhat to check
No feasible solutionThe medium, uptake bounds, objective, knockouts and maintenance requirement. For communities, also the abundances.
Internal fluxes change between runs or solversAlternative optima. Compare the objective values, and use pFBA or FVA.
FVA or pFVA cannot be chosenRun FBA, pFBA or Loopless first.
A reaction carries flux but is not on the mapIt is not drawn. Find it under Reactions & bounds, Not on map, and add it with the Reaction library.
An example does not loadYeast9 and the phenylalanine rescue need an internet connection to download their models.
Something elseReport it on GitHub issues, with the browser and a small example. Avoid posting unpublished or personal data.
